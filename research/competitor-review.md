# NZP: competitor research and website positioning

Reviewed 5 October 2026. Sources are primary company materials and announcements. Company claims are not independently validated performance data. This review compares technical routes and communication; it is not an exhaustive engineering diligence study.

## Recommended position

NZP develops modular Waste-to-Value projects around qualified feedstock, site infrastructure and local output demand. Syngas enables potential hydrogen, CO, power/heat and downstream synthesis routes. The website should make the project-development offering explicit, with modelling serving the development decision.

Syngas flexibility, high temperatures, modularity and the term waste-to-value are established category messages. They cannot support an exclusive differentiation claim on their own. NZP's defensible proposed distinction is the combination of its submerged-arc conversion architecture, industrial downstream modules, feedstock-led configuration and staged commercial development. Any superiority claim needs comparative operating evidence at equivalent system boundaries.

## 1. InEnTec — closest plasma-process comparator

Source: https://www.inentec.com/technology

Its PEM combines a DC plasma arc with an AC molten-glass environment. InEnTec describes syngas and vitrified products, with hydrogen, chemicals and fuels as potential uses. Its site includes application and facility descriptions, including Columbia Ridge.

Communication strength: it connects the conversion system to useful products and case studies. Its stated system architecture is specific enough for technical visitors to understand what is being offered.

Implication for NZP: plasma, high-temperature conversion, multiple output routes and vitrification are not unique claims. Explain the NZP architecture accurately and distinguish process operating conditions from arc temperatures. Avoid implying that a headline temperature establishes complete conversion, net efficiency or universal contaminant destruction.

Evidence to prepare: furnace architecture, electrical demand, electrode/refractory requirements, feedstock limits, contaminant fate, emissions, product quality and measured availability at the relevant scale. Direct comparisons need comparable duties and feedstocks.

## 2. Plagazi — integrated plasma-to-hydrogen proposition

Source: https://www.plagazi.com/technology/

Plagazi identifies InEnTec as its core plasma technology partner. The commercial narrative leads with circular hydrogen, then explains gas processing, hydrogen recovery and carbon capture/storage or utilisation. Other fuels are secondary pathways.

Communication strength: a specific end product and complete project process. Carbon capture is visibly part of the architecture rather than left implicit.

Implication for NZP: hydrogen needs its own customer route, expressed through purity, pressure, volume, supply continuity and delivery conditions. If NZP evaluates a sidecar supply model alongside existing reformers, describe the integration problem, not a universal claim of cheaper hydrogen. Renewable, low-carbon and carbon-negative labels require separate accounting and eligibility.

## 3. Sierra Energy — gasification-island licensing and qualification

Source: https://sierraenergy.com/technology/licensing/

FastOx is a different industrial gasification route. Sierra licenses the gasification island and clearly describes the project developer's upstream/downstream responsibilities. Its page states preparation considerations and offers a simple feedstock/size/end-product selector.

Communication strength: visitors can understand what is supplied, what is excluded and what information starts a licensing discussion.

Implication for NZP: publish responsibility boundaries. The NZP conversion core, gas cleaning, output conversion and developer services should not appear to be a single supplied turnkey scope by default. The project-screening tool should collect useful inputs and identify the next assessment, not invent bankable yields. Feedstock preparation must be assessed rather than universally dismissed.

## 4. SuMo / Sustainable Molecules — significant UK project competitor

Sources:
- https://www.eren-groupe.com/en/articles/sumo-launch-joint-venture-eren-groupe
- https://velocys.com/velocys-and-sumo-commission-fischer-tropsch-system-at-wednesbury-facility-advancing-uk-waste-to-saf-pathway/

Eren's February 2026 announcement presents SuMo's modular pressurised gasification platform and proposed UK/European waste-to-molecules rollout. Product routes include methanol, olefins, SAF, hydrogen and synthetic methane. Velocys separately reports FT-system commissioning at the Wednesbury facility and describes the integration as progression towards an end-to-end demonstration.

Communication strength: named project-development and synthesis relationships, a tangible facility, and explicit end products.

Implication for NZP: modular, configurable waste-to-molecules already has a close UK counterpart. NZP should compete on a well-defined project architecture and verifiable evidence, not broader slogans. Where partners can be named, state the exact scope and current stage. A commissioning milestone is not equivalent to a financed operating commercial plant.

## 5. Powerhouse Energy — UK modular syngas and feedstock testing

Sources:
- https://www.powerhouseenergy.co.uk/about-us/
- https://www.powerhouseenergy.co.uk/test-centre/

Powerhouse describes DMG as conversion of non-recyclable material to syngas and useful products. Its Bridgend test-centre page explicitly identifies the feedstock-testing unit as pre-commercial and offers testing and downstream-process trials.

Communication strength: feedstock qualification and the current scale of demonstration are tangible rather than hidden behind a generic proven claim.

Implication for NZP: separate component maturity, historical deployments and NZP integrated validation. An evidence page should say what the records substantiate and what remains project-specific. Initial screening is commercially more credible when it leads to an explicit validation programme.

## 6. Haffner Energy — output-led modular biomass competition

Sources:
- https://www.haffner-energy.com/renewable-hydrogen-solution-hynoca/
- https://www.haffner-energy.com/c-ic-documentation/
- https://www.haffner-energy.com/core100-update-new-integrated-multimodular-solutions-biofuels/

HYNOCA and SYNOCA address hydrogen and syngas requirements through a biomass-based route. The company organises product information by customer output and offers datasheets for modular configurations. Its September 2026 update describes expansion into integrated multi-module offerings.

Communication strength: clearly packaged output-specific products and supporting technical documents.

Implication for NZP: visitors should be able to navigate from required energy/molecules as well as feedstock. Biomass is a separate competitive segment; NZP's difficult-residual proposition must identify suitability, contamination limits, preparation and supply conditions. Modularity should be expressed as a practical configuration, not merely a visual or adjective.

## 7. GIDARA Energy — integrated gasification-to-fuels competition

Source: https://www.gidara-energy.com/

GIDARA leads with waste value, gas quality, industrial operating references and a downstream SAF technology alliance. Its HTW offering is a different gasification route. The site makes strong company claims about availability, pressure, operating hours, capital cost and conversion.

Communication strength: customer outcomes and delivery-chain credibility are presented together.

Implication for NZP: serious customers will expect specifications, balance-of-plant interfaces and reference evidence. Competitor marketing numbers must not be copied into NZP material or treated as independently comparable. Customer-specific net energy, raw/clean gas quality, preparation and downstream compression are more decision-useful than peak temperatures.

## 8. EQTEC — supplied reference and changed corporate context

Sources:
- User-supplied Introduction to EQTEC, September 2024 (historical reference).
- https://forgentplc.com/
- https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/761b47cf-9cfe-4351-b9b5-e4dce2f29a35.html

The supplied deck is useful for its industrial clarity, input/output architecture and separate treatment of commercial operations, team, applications and evidence. It is a September 2024 snapshot. Current EQTEC-domain traffic redirects to Forgent, whose site foregrounds critical metals. The January 2026 company announcement describes expansion beyond gasification.

Implication for NZP: use the reference's clarity rather than copying its design, plant imagery, claimed performance or corporate scale. Historical competitor presentations are not current commercial benchmarks. NZP needs its own evidence and accurately attributed imagery.

## Adjacent reference: Enerkem

Sources:
- https://enerkem.com/publications/enerkem-to-retire-its-edmonton-biofuels-facility
- https://enerkem.com/projects/edmonton

Enerkem's Edmonton material provides a waste-to-methanol/biofuels reference. Its retirement announcement is an important correction to treating historical plant launches as evidence of continuing operations. Avoid presenting retired facilities as current live assets.

## Launch decisions implemented

- Customer problem before technology; six focused solution pathways.
- Existing NZP logo and blue/cyan brand preserved.
- Original illustrative plant image, explicitly labelled; no competitor photos portrayed as NZP assets.
- Recycle → qualify → convert → clean/condition → configure process.
- Industrial furnace architecture and application-specific downstream equipment explained.
- Evidence classifications and project-specific validation visible.
- Staged screen/model/validate/develop/deploy process.
- Editable economics arithmetic, with no assumed NZP yield, carbon credit or guaranteed return.
- Project qualification, supporting attachments and downloadable brief.
- Existing NZP Formspree endpoint reused; successful UI only after an accepted response.
- No invented projects, partner logo wall, EPA approval transfer or unsupported destruction-efficiency claims.
